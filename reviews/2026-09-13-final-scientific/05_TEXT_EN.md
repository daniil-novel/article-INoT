# Independent English and scientific-text audit

## Coverage

I read the seven-page AAMAS PDF (`submission/aamas2027/paper/main.pdf`) end to end, including Figure 1, all tables, limitations, conclusion, and references; I checked its TeX sources and included English section files. I also read the converted Springer PDF (`converted_article_springer.pdf`, 111 pages) sequentially through the prose, methods, appendices, legends, complete outcome tables, and bibliography. The long appendix tables were checked for legend and template consistency rather than manually recounting every numeric cell. The PDF text extraction is typographically noisy at line wraps, so wording findings below refer to source TeX phrases and file locations.

## Findings

1. **Terminology drifts between role names.** The AAMAS body opens with “a planner, implementer, and critic” (`submission/aamas2027/paper/body.tex:2`), while the experimental labels and later description use “planner, implementer, reviewer” (`body.tex:43`). The Springer introduction similarly says “planner, worker and critic” (`sections/heldout_context_en.tex:3`) and later settles on planner/implementer/reviewer. This is a real readability and construct-validity problem: readers can interpret critic and reviewer as different interventions. Use one canonical trio everywhere, preferably “planner, implementer, and reviewer,” and state once that these are prompt labels for instructed functions.

2. **“Quality–resource tradeoff” implies a joint estimand that the paper explicitly does not define.** The phrase occurs in the converted introduction (“improve the quality–resource tradeoff relative to distributing them across three calls”; `converted_article_springer.tex` via `sections/heldout_context_en.tex`/revision text). Later passages say that no validated weighted score, quality-loss margin, or joint quality–cost decision exists (`sections/heldout_context_en.tex:25`, `sections/revision_en.tex:31`). Replace with “improve verified quality while reducing measured resource use” or “change verified quality and measured resource use.” This removes an apparent contradiction.

3. **The headline SCC sentence is grammatically and scientifically ambiguous.** The Springer abstract says “A separate amended SCC comparison completes 2,000 assigned task-repeat blocks across 956 tasks (6,000 method assignments)” (`sections/revision_en.tex:5`). The methods clarify that the amendment selects/fixes the first 2,000 blocks, while only 5,595 workflows are complete and 405 assignments remain incomplete (`sections/standalone_details_en.tex:59`). Suggested replacement: “A separate amended SCC comparison fixes the first 2,000 task-repeat blocks (6,000 method assignments) across 956 tasks; 5,595 workflows are complete, and the remaining outcomes are unavailable.” The same correction should be applied to the AAMAS contribution sentence (`submission/aamas2027/paper/body.tex:10`) if it is intended to describe completed data.

4. **“Task,” “task cluster,” and “task-level unit” are not consistently introduced.** The revised Springer abstract and results use “1,000 BigCodeBench task clusters” (`sections/revision_en.tex:5`; `sections/scale1000_results_en.tex:4`), whereas the AAMAS paper and most prose say “tasks.” If “cluster” is only a synonym for one BigCodeBench task ID, define it at first use and use “task ID” or “task” consistently. If it denotes a grouping, explain the distinction before reporting task-weighted estimands.

5. **Several sentences repeat the same caveat in near-identical form.** “It does not establish a general quality advantage of role names,” “does not establish a general claim about multi-agent systems,” “does not show that separate agents are generally ineffective,” and “does not isolate a universal benefit of role names” recur in the abstract, related work, results, discussion, and limitations (`body.tex:8,17,115,137`; `sections/scale1000_results_en.tex:33`; `sections/heldout_limitations_en.tex:11`). The caveat is correct, but repetition makes the paper sound defensive. State the scope once in the abstract and once in the limitations, then use shorter local references such as “This protocol-specific result.”

6. **“Native evaluation” is technically meaningful but opaque to general readers.** It appears repeatedly in the AAMAS abstract and body (`submission/aamas2027/paper/body.tex:71,73,100,137`) and in the Springer appendices. Define it once as “execution of the original benchmark tests in the pinned offline environment,” then prefer “benchmark-test evaluation” or “original-test evaluation” in narrative prose. Keep “native” in table labels only if it is part of the recorded field name.

7. **A few constructions are formal but unnatural English.** Examples include “the contribution here is the specified architectural evaluation” (`sections/heldout_context_en.tex:18`), “the design never treats a model’s self-critique as the measured verification result” (converted introduction), and “the quality–resource tradeoff” (above). Suggested edits: “Our contribution is an auditable evaluation of this architecture”; “We measure correctness with external tests, not with the model’s self-critique”; and “the relation between verified quality and measured resource use.” These are clearer without changing claims.

8. **The appendix legends are broadly consistent, with one presentation risk.** Repeated legends alternate “G = no complete candidate,” “G = incomplete generation,” and “G = incomplete generation; N = missing native outcome,” depending on the table family. The distinctions are valid, but the same letter should not change meaning across tables without a table-specific sentence. Use a global status legend or explicitly label the two schemas as “initial-study status” and “Luna-study status.”

9. **Date metadata for pricing is inconsistent.** The main AAMAS body says rates were checked on 13 September 2026 (`submission/aamas2027/paper/body.tex:94`), while the bibliography entry says the pricing page was accessed on 8 September 2026 (`sections/revision_references.tex:15`) and a development section also says rates were accessed on 8 September (`sections/codex_pilot_en.tex:18`). Align the access date with the actual check used for each tariff, or state that the initial and Luna studies used different tariff snapshots.

10. **Bibliography verification.** On 13 September 2026 I checked 20 of 21 bibliography entries against primary/official pages: arXiv records for the research papers, NeurIPS proceedings, ACL Anthology, official SWE-bench documentation, official OpenAI documentation/pricing, the official GitHub repository for the SCC software, and the publisher/official record for the econometrics paper. All 20 checked records exist and match the cited title/authors at the level needed for this audit. Entry [2] is now identifiable through the official author repository but should include its URL in the manuscript. The Journal of Econometrics DOI page returned a web-tool error during direct opening, but the official publisher search result and author-hosted record confirm the title, journal, volume, pages, and DOI. This is a source-access limitation, not evidence of a false citation. The AAMAS seven-page bibliography and Springer bibliography use different numbering and should be rechecked after any reference consolidation.

## Editorial recommendation

The English is generally intelligible and appropriately cautious for a methods paper. The priority pass is terminology normalization (critic/worker/reviewer; task/task cluster), removal of the joint-tradeoff implication, and one careful rewrite of the SCC completion sentence. After that, consolidate repeated scope caveats and normalize status legends and access dates. Nothing in this language audit supports an inference about AI authorship; stylistic regularity is a quality observation only.
