# AI assistance in study development

AI assistance was used to develop and revise hypotheses, experimental design,
evaluation code, statistical reporting and prose. It was not limited to copy
editing. The human author is responsible for the submitted work. Author-side
AI critiques are development aids, not reviews commissioned by AAMAS.

## Verification and provenance limits

Executable checks verify assignment identities, generation and usage records,
native evaluation joins, source-code extraction and the stated numerical
analyses. The final report of each check must distinguish a completed check
from a planned one. Software tests alone do not validate a scientific claim;
the author-side review records retain methodological and editorial findings.

The selected-input record is not a complete transcript of study development.
The inspected historical archive retains some delegated payloads in opaque
form, without recoverable verbatim prompt text. Inherited manuscript context,
other messages and earlier unavailable inputs are not reconstructed here.
This provenance gap is disclosed rather than filled with invented prompts.
The experimentally delivered instructions and the final statistical procedure
are specified independently in the scientific methods and evidence files.

No claim of preregistration follows from this disclosure. The SCC amendment's
timing is reported explicitly. No language check or AI detector can establish
that a text was wholly written by a human; the purpose of editorial review is
to improve clarity, correctness and consistency.
