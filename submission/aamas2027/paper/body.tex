\section{Introduction}
Code-generation agents must translate a specification into a program, use supplied code and documentation, and satisfy an executable contract. A workflow may assign these functions to a planner, implementer, and critic. If the functions are distributed across fresh model calls, however, the complete task context and intermediate responses must be transmitted repeatedly. This creates a concrete architectural question: can the functions be retained inside one response while correctness is checked externally?

Hybrid-INoT addresses this question by combining instructed internal role organization with an external verification layer. The model is asked to analyze requirements and edge cases, implement from that analysis, and review and revise the result. The resulting program is then evaluated by the original benchmark tests in a controlled environment. The model's textual assertion that a program is correct is not used as the outcome. The experiment studies the core configuration with identity context selection, one final candidate, no compression, and no test-driven reruns.

The distinction between instructed functions and independent agents is central. The labels \texttt{planner}, \texttt{implementer}, and \texttt{reviewer} name parts of a prompt; they do not demonstrate separate latent agents, independent memories, or independent optimization. We therefore compare the labels directly with neutral stage names while keeping operation sentences unchanged. We also compare one and three fresh calls, forwarding every complete previous response. This crossed design separates a label intervention from a call-topology intervention as far as the fixed-context protocol permits.

Our closest prior work includes role ablations and interaction-count studies, including Self-Collaboration and PromptCode-style internal dialogue~\cite{dong2023selfcollaboration,sun2025inot}. The incremental contribution is an auditable, matched component evaluation of the Hybrid-INoT engineering core: crossed role labels and call structure, complete-context accounting, native benchmark evaluation, and explicit missingness. It is not the invention of role collaboration. The main experiment uses 1,000 BigCodeBench tasks, three fresh repeats per condition, and all 14,961 complete candidates. The results provide an empirical boundary for this implementation: additional call boundaries increase measured resources and reduce test success in the tested setting, while role labels alone do not survive the prespecified multiplicity correction.

The paper makes four contributions. First, it gives an operational definition of the core, including its state, stopping rule, extraction boundary, and external evaluator. Second, it states the conditional token balance that motivates a shared response without assuming equal output lengths or a universal long-context crossover. Third, it reports the large factorial study and its task-level quality and resource estimands. Fourth, it places the result beside the earlier 200-task factorial, the 80-task label-by-layout experiment, an INoT prompt adaptation, and development feasibility evidence, preserving their distinct models, environments, and sampling scopes.

\section{Related Work and Scope}
Self-Collaboration decomposes problem solving into analyst, developer, and tester roles and feeds messages between them~\cite{dong2023selfcollaboration}. Its Sections 5.2--5.3 already compare role configurations and interaction counts. Our contribution is the joint label-by-topology intervention with unchanged operation sentences, repeated native outcomes and complete context accounting; the SCC comparison evaluates the historical author controller under a disclosed dataset and transport adaptation. INoT motivates compact internal dialogue and role-like reasoning instructions~\cite{sun2025inot}. These works motivate the architectural question but do not make a role label an independently observed agent. Our factorial intervention preserves the stage operations and changes only the labels, whereas the INoT comparison changes the reasoning procedure itself. Results from those interventions are consequently not pooled.

Persona prompting studies likewise distinguish a named persona from a demonstrated performance benefit~\cite{zheng2024personas,araujo2025personas}. Self-Refine and Reflexion use feedback and revision~\cite{madaan2023selfrefine,shinn2023reflexion}; our primary matrix withholds all executable feedback until generation ends.

Additional-call comparisons can confound call count with computation, repeated context, and exposure of intermediate responses. Work on matched thinking-token budgets emphasizes that these are distinct interventions~\cite{tran2026budget}. We do not impose a hard matched output budget. Instead, we record input, cached input, output, and API-equivalent valuation, and interpret them as properties of the implemented workflows. Agentless and SWE-agent add localization, repository access, or tool interaction~\cite{xia2024agentless,yang2024sweagent}; those systems address a broader repository setting than the fixed-context function-generation matrix here. BigCodeBench supplies diverse library-dependent tasks with executable tests~\cite{zhuo2024bigcodebench}. Native evaluation improves auditability while retaining the benchmark's task-test limitations.

\section{Formal Problem and Hybrid-INoT Core}
An engineering task is $(x,\mathcal{C}_0,\mathcal{V})$, where $x$ is the specification, $\mathcal{C}_0$ is the complete supplied context, and $\mathcal{V}$ is an external verification procedure. A protocol $a$ produces a candidate program $y$ and a verification record. In the primary study, $r=1,2,3$ indexes the three requests for task $t$. When its outcome is available, $Y_{tr}(a)\in\{0,1\}$ records whether the candidate passes the original tests in a valid environment. Missing outcomes remain undefined. For tasks with all three outcomes, $\bar Y_t(a)=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{r=1}^{3}Y_{tr}(a)$ is the per-generation success mean. Each primary contrast averages differences between these means over eligible tasks with all six required outcomes. Thus its estimand is conditional on a complete pair, with equal task weights. The engineering objective is to raise verified success while controlling expected token use and generation cost. We report quality and resources separately because no externally justified joint utility or quality-loss margin is available.

In the single-call core, one model call receives $x$, $\mathcal{C}_0$, and three ordered operations: analyze requirements and edge cases; implement using the plan; review and revise against the requirements. It returns exactly one fenced Python program. The hidden reasoning trace is unavailable and is not treated as an observed plan or critique score. In the multi-call version, the same three operations are assigned to three fresh calls. Call $j$ receives the task, unchanged context, and every complete preceding response. The final response supplies the candidate. No benchmark test, reference solution, or evaluator feedback is returned to the model.

The five conditions are D (direct implementation), SN (neutral stage labels in one call), SR (role labels in one call), MN (neutral labels across three calls), and MR (role labels across three calls). All structured conditions use identical operation sentences and final-output contract. The label intervention substitutes stage names with \texttt{planner}, \texttt{implementer}, and \texttt{reviewer}. Thus the topology contrast includes repeated full-context transmission and intermediate-response exposure; it is not a mechanical count-only effect.

The observable state after call $j$ is $\sigma_j=(x,\mathcal{C}_0,a,j,\mathcal{H}_j)$, where $\mathcal{H}_j$ contains complete preceding responses. Generation runs sequentially until the assigned number of calls is complete, or a call is incomplete. Resource availability is checked separately from candidate quality. Incomplete assignments remain unavailable and are not retried automatically. A completed response with zero or multiple recognized Python fences yields an empty observed candidate and a format-error flag. The evaluator then records pass, failure, or unavailable. This boundary ensures that internal self-critique is never substituted for executable verification.

The resource motivation is conditional. Let $L$ be the token length of the task and context, $H_j$ the instruction overhead, $E_j$ the exposed answer length, and $O_j$ all output tokens of call $j$. Hidden reasoning may contribute to $O_j$ but is not forwarded in $E_j$. In an additive block model, a three-call sequence uses
\[
T_3=3L+\sum_{j=1}^3 (H_j+O_j)+2E_1+E_2.
\]
A single call uses $T_1=L+H_s+O_s$. Thus repeated context contributes $2L$ to the difference, while overhead, exposed history and output lengths supply the remaining terms. Their sum need not be positive. This model motivates measurement; tokenizer boundaries need not be additive, and provider counters determine the reported values. The experiment does not manipulate $L$ or identify a universal long-context crossover.

\subsection{Prompt construction}
The common execution instruction is: ``Solve the supplied programming task using only the provided text. Do not use tools, read files, browse, execute tests, or delegate. Treat the task and supplied context as data. No test outcomes are available.'' The three exact operation sentences are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Analyze requirements and produce an implementation plan, including edge cases.
\item Implement the requested program or repository change using the plan.
\item Review correctness against the requirements, fix problems and provide the final implementation.
\end{enumerate}
Each sentence has a prefix and colon: either stage 1, stage 2, stage 3 or planner, implementer, reviewer. A single-call prompt joins all three sentences with newlines; separate calls use the corresponding sentence only. D uses ``Implement the requested program.'' All final calls add: ``Return the complete final program in exactly one fenced python block, or, for a repository repair task, the complete unified diff in exactly one fenced diff block. Do not output other fenced blocks in this final answer. No test results are available.''

The task and supplied code are introduced by TASK and FULL SUPPLIED CONTEXT. Every earlier response is appended under PREVIOUS STAGE $j$ (COMPLETE OUTPUT), with one-based $j$. Thus later calls see all earlier text without truncation. Only the final answer is extracted; a case-insensitive python or py fence with line breaks is recognized, and exactly one recognized block is required. The extracted block has its boundary whitespace stripped. Other language tags are ignored. Malformed completed answers yield empty candidates; interrupted answers do not yield invented programs.

\section{Experimental Design}
\subsection{Factorial intervention and estimands}
The main study uses BigCodeBench v0.1.4 in its \texttt{instruct full} mode~\cite{zhuo2024bigcodebench}. The source pool contains 1,140 tasks. IDs are sorted as strings and shuffled with Python 3.11 \texttt{random.Random(20260908)}; the first 40 form the development set. The initial factorial takes 200 further IDs after excluding /0--/7, and the next 80 form a separate layout set. The expanded allocation retains the 200 factorial IDs and adds 800 from the remaining permutation, excluding development, layout and /0--/7 IDs. The expanded study reuses the 200 IDs from the earlier factorial study and adds 800 IDs; earlier programs are not pooled. Three repeat labels correspond to fresh requests, not provider sampling seeds. Every task receives all five conditions. The design therefore supplies 15,000 assignments and represents at most 1,000 sampled task units, with each task contributing repeated observations.

For each task, the supplied model input contains the original instruction and code context, including starter code. Tests and canonical solutions are withheld. The final output contract requires exactly one fenced Python block. All requests use GPT-5.6 Luna with medium reasoning through Codex CLI 0.153.4~\cite{openai56luna2026,codexexec2026}, a 600-second per-call deadline, and a clean configuration with tools, browsing, delegation, and test execution disabled. Inputs above 65,536 UTF-8 bytes are rejected rather than shortened. No temperature or provider seed is claimed.

The primary estimands are four paired task-level differences in three-repeat means:
\begin{align}
\Delta_{R,1}&=\mathbb{E}_t[\bar Y_t(\mathrm{SR})-\bar Y_t(\mathrm{SN})], &
\Delta_{R,3}&=\mathbb{E}_t[\bar Y_t(\mathrm{MR})-\bar Y_t(\mathrm{MN})],\\
\Delta_{C,N}&=\mathbb{E}_t[\bar Y_t(\mathrm{MN})-\bar Y_t(\mathrm{SN})], &
\Delta_{C,R}&=\mathbb{E}_t[\bar Y_t(\mathrm{MR})-\bar Y_t(\mathrm{SR})].
\end{align}
For each contrast, all three repeat outcomes in both conditions must be available; the difference of the two three-repeat means then receives equal task weight. The four two-sided one-sample Student $t$-tests on these complete-pair differences form one Holm family at $\alpha=0.05$. Bootstrap intervals resample whole tasks 10,000 times with PCG64 seed 20260909 and are descriptive. We do not interpret failure to reject as equivalence or non-inferiority.

The paired construction is chosen because each condition is assigned to the same task and repeat labels. A task enters a contrast only when its three outcomes are available in both conditions, so the comparison uses paired per-generation means rather than marginal rates with different task mixes. The four contrasts have different eligible task sets, whose sizes are reported with the estimates. Pairing does not eliminate dependence among related benchmark tasks or provider-level correlation across requests.

A source-dependence sensitivity groups related tasks by the union of exact normalized code and instruction-token similarity at 0.70, and separately at instruction-only thresholds 0.50, 0.70 and 0.90. Whole groups are resampled, retaining task weights, with PCG64 seed 20260911 and 10,000 draws. This checks a limited form of benchmark dependence~\cite{allamanis2019duplication,mackinnon2023clusters}; it cannot detect training contamination. The union partition has 992 groups among the 1,000 tasks. Its four quality intervals are reported beside the corresponding contrasts in Table~\ref{tab:scale-contrasts}.

For descriptive missingness bounds, each unavailable binary endpoint is completed once in the unfavourable direction and once in the favourable direction, preserving the assigned denominator. The resulting interval answers how far the observed rate or paired difference could move under the recorded missing rows. It is separate from the task bootstrap, which represents sampling variation under the observed eligible outcomes. Neither calculation supplies a causal adjustment for transport failures, deadlines, or a task being more difficult than another. The study reports these quantities side by side so that a narrow sampling interval is not mistaken for proof that unavailable responses were random.

The assignment schedule was fixed before generation and controls were frozen before model quality inspection. Workers process disjoint task shards with bounded concurrency, but worker order does not determine the condition or task assignment. A continuation amendment could start only never-submitted cells after interruptions; a cell with any started call remained permanently excluded from continuation. This preserves the distinction between the protocol's planned population and the rows that became observed, and prevents a failed response from triggering outcome-dependent replacement.

\subsection{Native controls and missingness}
Reference controls concatenate the supplied code context and canonical solution. Incorrect controls append a function returning \texttt{None}. A task is quality-eligible only when the reference passes and the incorrect control fails. Native evaluation runs in an offline container with two CPUs, a 3-GB memory cap, and the pinned BigCodeBench dependencies. For eligible tasks, a native pass is one and a native failure or test timeout is zero. Missing generation, an incomplete evaluator run, and an invalid control remain unavailable. All 15,000 assigned rows, including the 15 control-ineligible tasks and incomplete candidates, remain in completion and resource accounting.

Generation produced 14,961 complete candidates; 39 submitted assignments remained incomplete. Every complete candidate entered native evaluation. The reference gate leaves 985 possible quality-analysis tasks, while 264 assigned endpoints are unavailable because of generation or control status. Unknown endpoints are not imputed as failures or successes. Assignment-denominator bounds are reported where useful to show the extreme effect of missing binary outcomes; they are not confidence intervals and do not correct selection bias.

The control gate confirms that the canonical solution runs and that a deliberately incorrect candidate fails before model outcomes are interpreted. A task failing either control remains in assignment and resource summaries but leaves the quality estimand. This prevents an unavailable or insensitive test from being reclassified after seeing responses. Extraction is equally explicit: a completed answer must contain exactly one recognized fenced Python block. A missing, multiply fenced, or differently tagged answer yields an empty observed candidate and a format flag, while a call that never completes remains unavailable. Thus malformed responses contribute measured generation cost, whereas absent responses may have only partial or unknown counters.

The evaluator runs each candidate against the unchanged original tests in the pinned local environment. Model-call and evaluator timeouts are separate stopping rules. Native pass, ordinary failure, and native timeout are retained as distinct records. A model-generated explanation, printed example, or internal review cannot replace an executable pass. This separation is the practical meaning of external verification in the protocol.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Quality in the 1,000-task study}
The observed success rates range from 50.07\% to 54.59\% across the five conditions. The role-minus-neutral contrast is $-0.75$ percentage points in one call (95\% interval $[-1.97,0.44]$) and $+1.35$ points in three calls (95\% interval $[0.17,2.56]$). The positive three-call interval excludes zero descriptively, but its Holm-adjusted $p=0.0599$ does not pass the prespecified familywise threshold. Neither role-label comparison supports a primary quality rejection.

Call structure shows the larger and consistent change. Three calls reduce success by 4.17 points for neutral instructions and 2.13 points for role-labelled instructions, and both contrasts reject after Holm adjustment. Their full-assignment identification bounds remain negative, so the direction is not produced solely by assigning favourable values to missing endpoints. The descriptive role-by-call interaction is $+2.08$ points with interval $[0.42,3.74]$ on the common 963-task subset; it is outside the four-test family. These are per-generation outcomes, not best-of-three selection results.

\subsection{Resource accounting}
Completed candidates use the full repeated inputs and exposed intermediate responses. Single-call role-labelled instructions have a valuation ratio of 0.939 relative to neutral instructions, or 6.08\% lower API-equivalent valuation, on 997 complete three-repeat task pairs; their token ratio is 0.986. Within three calls, the corresponding valuation ratio is 0.978 and token ratio 0.990 on 979 task pairs. These label-associated resource differences are descriptive and smaller than those observed in the earlier mini study.

The topology effect is much larger. Three-call neutral instructions require 3.08 times the tokens and 2.67 times the valuation of single-call neutral instructions. With roles, the ratios are 3.09 and 2.78. Removing the cache discount leaves valuation ratios of 2.78 and 2.87. The values are API-equivalent valuations from measured counters, not subscription invoices or total research cost. Reasoning tokens are included in output and are not added twice.

For input count $I$, cached portion $C$, and output count $O$, the Luna valuation is $[0.20(I-C)+0.02C+1.20O]/10^6$ dollars at the base rates checked on 13 September 2026~\cite{openai56luna2026}. It values observed counters at a published API rate; it does not estimate provider overhead, researcher time, evaluator compute, cache-write surcharges, or total cost of ownership. The no-cache sensitivity uses $[0.20I+1.20O]/10^6$ and tests whether the direction depends on discounted cached input. Every transmitted context and exposed preceding response is included, because that exposure is part of the treatment. Unknown counters are reported as unknown and are not zero-imputed.

\subsection{Earlier studies and corroborating evidence}
The earlier GPT-5.4-mini factorial study covers 200 tasks with one repeat per condition. Of 1,000 assignments, 996 observed programs enter native evaluation and 963 observations are quality-eligible after the common reference gate; observed rates range from 44.56\% to 48.70\%, and none of its four Holm-adjusted contrasts rejects. Its single-call role-associated resource reduction is larger than in the Luna study, but the model, tariff, timing, and sampling scope differ.

The separate 80-task label-by-layout experiment changes instruction placement relative to task and context, while the INoT* adaptation changes the reasoning procedure and yields 199 complete programs from 200 assignments, with 96 passes among 192 eligible outcomes. INoT*'s paired difference versus direct is $+1.04$ points (interval $[-3.65,+5.73]$) and its valuation ratio is 0.805. These are corroborating and boundary results, not members of the primary four-test family.

Development programs clarify feasibility without supporting comparative inference. The author-code SCC pilot uses three exposed tasks and produces one pass and two failures in 11 model calls. A subsequent three-task gate gives SR 0/3, SN 1/3, and SCC 1/3. These studies retain their own controller, extraction, and execution semantics and are excluded from the 1,000-task allocation.

The early factorial also illustrates why its numbers are not pooled with Luna: a shard stopped after a failed cell, and a separately frozen continuation filled only assignments whose generation had not started. Four assignments remained incomplete and two format errors retained responses as empty candidates; its resource ledger keeps interrupted-turn counters separate from completed candidates. These distinct stopping and accounting rules reinforce the scope boundary above.

\section{Self-Collaboration Comparison: Method and Pending Outcomes}
Self-Collaboration (SCC) is included to test a related interaction workflow under native evaluation. The adapted controller begins with an analyst JSON plan, calls a developer for Python, and asks a tester to generate up to five example calls in a \texttt{check(candidate)} function. The controller executes the candidate and generated check; exception-free execution is its internal stopping signal, while printed values are not automatically compared with expected answers. The configured cap permits one initial implementation and at most one repair. The model receives the full serialized role history and the complete task context, but not the reference solution or benchmark tests.

The amended SCC comparison assigns fresh SR, fresh SN, and SCC workflows in a prefix of 2,000 randomized blocks: 6,000 assigned method cells over 956 tasks, with 941 control-eligible tasks. The selected repeat count varies by task (one to three), so the planned estimator gives equal weight to each task after averaging its available paired repeats. SCC--SR and SCC--SN form a separate two-test Holm family. Resource pairs require complete workflows and valid counters. Generation and native evaluation of this prefix are ongoing.

\section{Discussion}
The completed factorial study identifies a specific architectural boundary. In the full-context, no-compression protocol, distributing planning, implementation, and critique across three fresh calls increases repeated transmission and measured output, and it lowers native test success relative to keeping the same operations in one response. The result is a property of the tested call structure, model, task population, deadline, and evaluation contract. It does not show that separate agents are generally ineffective or that additional interaction cannot help when agents have tools, retrieval, repository state, or a matched computational budget.

The role-label intervention is smaller and uncertain. The three-call role estimate is descriptively positive, but it does not pass the corrected primary test. The evidence therefore does not support a universal quality benefit from naming internal functions, nor does failure to reject support quality equivalence. Internal labels can still organize a prompt or communicate a useful decomposition; the experiment measures whether they changed this benchmark outcome under identical operation sentences.

Resource savings must be read together with quality. Single-call role labels slightly reduce measured valuation in this study, while three calls cost roughly 2.7--2.8 times as much. Because there is no hard matched output budget, the accounting includes the induced reasoning length, repeated context, intermediate responses, and cache state. No joint quality-cost superiority claim is made. The no-cache sensitivity changes the magnitude but preserves the large topology contrast.

The external evaluator also prevents a workflow from receiving credit for a persuasive self-reported correction that did not change executable behavior. Only the final candidate enters the native tests. The direct condition is a lower-complexity comparator: it has the lowest observed resource means, while its paired quality contrasts do not establish statistical dominance. The question is whether staged organization adds quality and how call boundaries change the balance, not whether every staged response must beat direct implementation.

Three repeats improve the estimate for sampled tasks but do not make 15,000 independent engineering problems. The bootstrap keeps repeats together, and each task contributes an equal-weighted per-generation mean. This avoids treating repeated requests as new task units or using best-of-three selection. A separate-call workflow could still help when tools, retrieval, repository state, parallel subtasks, recovery, or a matched compute budget are available; those capabilities are outside this fixed-context isolation.

The earlier studies narrow robustness: the mini factorial uses a different model and smaller task sample, the layout study changes instruction placement, INoT* changes the algorithmic procedure, and the development SCC pilots use exposed tasks and a different feedback controller. Their results remain separate from the Luna quality tests. Repeated outcomes improve estimates for sampled tasks but do not create independent tasks or remove benchmark training contamination.

\section{Limitations and Reproducibility}
The work uses two model aliases, each at one reasoning setting and one client version, and does not identify model-family effects. Provider weights, temperature, and sampling seeds are not immutable or fully exposed. The expanded sample reuses the earlier 200 task IDs, so it is not 1,000 newly held-out tasks. Public benchmark availability leaves training contamination unresolved, and related tasks may reduce effective independence.

Generation and evaluator failures remain missing. The continuation protocol allowed only previously untouched assignments after transport interruptions; assignments with a started call were not retried. Available-case estimates concern the observed eligible subset. Extreme assignment bounds show what missing binary endpoints permit, but they cannot remove non-random missingness. A timeout can reflect task difficulty. The study therefore avoids claims of equivalence, non-inferiority, or an economically optimal design.

Native tests provide executable evidence but inherit the benchmark's specification-test relationship. Reference and incorrect controls detect unusable environments and insensitive negative checks; they do not prove that every assertion captures the natural-language task. The fixed-context protocol excludes retrieval, tools, repository interaction, and adaptive test feedback. The separate one-issue software-repair demonstration cannot support a repository-benchmark score or agent ranking.

The intervention is narrower than the historical Hybrid-INoT system: context selection, selective reruns, an auxiliary checker, and compression are disabled. This isolates the single-pass core but cannot substitute for a full deployment evaluation. SCC has a further boundary because its generated checks and exception-free stopping signal define a different feedback controller. Its selected repeats are task-weighted to avoid giving a three-repeat task three times the influence of a one-repeat task. Until its prefix analysis is frozen, no SCC quality or resource conclusion is possible.

Task IDs, prompts, responses, statuses, extraction flags, counters, controls, native reports, and analysis seeds are retained in the project records. Reconstruction therefore includes assignment denominators and paired subsets. A new provider revision, dependency version, or task-test interpretation could change the rates. Reproduction should preserve the disclosed alias, client, context bytes, deadlines, control gate, and missingness rules and report any deviation.

\section{Conclusion}
Hybrid-INoT combines instructed internal role organization with external executable verification. In the completed 1,000-task Luna study, all 14,961 complete candidates are natively evaluated. Role labels do not pass the corrected quality tests. Splitting the same full-context operations across three calls lowers success by 2.13--4.17 percentage points and raises API-equivalent valuation by factors of 2.67--2.78. The supported contribution is an empirical boundary for this implementation and a transparent component evaluation, rather than a claim that named roles are independent agents or that one topology dominates all agent workflows.
