Language model workflows often combine named roles with repeated calls, making the effects of these choices difficult to separate. We crossed neutral versus planner, implementer and reviewer labels with one versus three calls, keeping requested operations and supplied context fixed. A direct solver provided a fifth condition. GPT-5.6 Luna received 15,000 assignments on 1,000 programming tasks, with three fresh requests per condition; 14,961 candidates completed generation and 985 tasks passed evaluation controls. Task-paired success changed by -0.75 and +1.35 percentage points with role labels; neither label test rejected after Holm correction, which does not establish equivalence. Three calls reduced executable success by 4.17 points under neutral labels and 2.13 points under role labels. Their API-equivalent valuations were 2.67 and 2.78 times those of the corresponding single-call workflows on complete resource pairs. Source-dependence and newly sampled-task checks preserved the negative call-structure direction. An auxiliary controller comparison remained inconclusive at the full-population level because of missing outcomes. These results support testing simple baselines before expanding fixed-context generation workflows; they do not establish a general effect of independent agents or equal-compute systems.
