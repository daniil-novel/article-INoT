\title{Separating Role Labels from Call Structure in LLM Code Generation: An Empirical Quality--Resource Study}
\author[hse]{Daniil Privezentsev\corref{cor1}}
\ead{daprivezentsev@edu.hse.ru}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\address[hse]{National Research University Higher School of Economics, 20 Myasnitskaya Ulitsa, Moscow 101000, Russia}

\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Context:} Software teams choosing LLM code-generation workflows need evidence on executable quality and resource use. Named roles and extra calls are often introduced together, obscuring their separate effects.

\textbf{Objectives:} We distinguish lexical role labels from one- versus three-call execution while keeping requested operations and full task context unchanged.

\textbf{Methods:} A crossed experiment compares neutral and planner--implementer--reviewer labels in one or three calls, alongside a direct solver. GPT-5.6 Luna receives three fresh requests per condition on 1,000 BigCodeBench tasks: 15,000 assignments, 14,961 completed generations, and 985 control-eligible tasks. Task-paired analyses separate quality, provider-observed resources, and missingness. An earlier GPT-5.4-mini study and an adapted Self-Collaboration comparison provide bounded corroboration.

\textbf{Results:} Role-label differences are $-0.75$ percentage points in one call and $+1.35$ points in three calls; neither null is rejected after Holm correction. This does not establish equivalence. Three calls reduce paired success by 4.17 and 2.13 points under neutral and role labels, respectively, while API-equivalent valuation increases by factors of 2.67 and 2.78 on complete resource pairs. The topology direction persists in the 800 newly sampled task IDs and the source-dependence audit. Adapted Self-Collaboration has lower success on observed pairs and approximately three times the valuation, but its missing-outcome bounds permit either quality ranking.

\textbf{Conclusion:} For these full-context function-generation workflows, additional calls require empirical justification. Direct and single-call staged prompts provide useful low-resource baselines; neither a general multi-agent penalty nor a universal deployment optimum follows.
\end{abstract}
