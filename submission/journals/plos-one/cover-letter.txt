Dear Editors of PLOS One,

Please consider “Role labels and repeated calls in large language model code generation: a task-paired experimental study” as a Research Article. The study separates role labels from repeated model calls in a controlled, task-paired evaluation of executable code generation. Its contribution is the unchanged-operation factorial design and explicit accounting of task dependence, missing outcomes and measured resources.

The primary experiment comprises 1,000 programming tasks and 15,000 assigned generations. Repeated calls lower executable success on observed eligible pairs and increase API-equivalent resource valuation. Role-label tests do not reject after multiple-comparison correction; the manuscript does not interpret those results as equivalence. We report the external-controller comparison conditionally because missing outcomes permit either full-population ordering.

The work fits PLOS One's evaluation of technically sound research irrespective of the direction of results. Protocols, assignment outcomes, primary programs, evaluator records and analysis software are supplied in a frozen replication archive. The data statement identifies omitted materials and the unverified status of a fresh native rebuild. The manuscript documents substantive AI assistance and author responsibility.
