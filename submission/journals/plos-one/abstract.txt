Named roles and repeated model calls are often combined in language model workflows, making their separate contributions difficult to assess. We crossed neutral versus planner, implementer and reviewer labels with one versus three calls while holding operation sentences and supplied context fixed. A direct solver provided a fifth condition. The primary model, GPT-5.6 Luna, received 15,000 assignments across 1,000 BigCodeBench programming tasks and three fresh requests per condition. Generation completed for 14,961 assignments; 985 tasks passed reference and incorrect-program controls. Analysis used task-paired per-generation success, retaining repeats within tasks. Role labels changed success by -0.75 percentage points in one call and +1.35 points in three calls; neither null hypothesis was rejected after Holm correction, which does not establish equivalence. Three calls reduced success by 4.17 points under neutral labels and 2.13 points under role labels. On complete resource pairs, their API-equivalent valuations were 2.67 and 2.78 times those of the corresponding single-call workflows. The negative call-structure estimates persisted in source-dependence and newly sampled-task analyses. An earlier study with a related model showed the same contrast directions on 200 reused task identifiers but provided no independent task replication. A separate published-controller comparison was limited by substantial infrastructure missingness and did not support a full-population quality ranking. Within the tested full-context workflows, repeated calls increased resource use and lowered executable success on observed eligible pairs. The design does not establish a general effect of independent agents, equal-compute systems or repository-level development.
