\title[Role Labels and Call Topology]{Role Labels or Extra Calls? A Controlled Study of Quality and Cost in LLM Code Generation}

\begin{abstract}
LLM code-generation workflows often combine named roles with multiple model calls, making it unclear which design choice changes quality and resource use. We disentangle these choices in a crossed experiment: neutral versus planner--implementer--reviewer labels, and one versus three calls, with identical operations and full task context; a direct solver is a fifth condition. GPT-5.6 Luna received 15,000 assignments on 1,000 BigCodeBench tasks, with three fresh requests per condition. Generation completed for 14,961 assignments, and 985 tasks passed the reference controls. Role labels change success by $-0.75$ percentage points in one call and $+1.35$ points in three calls; neither null hypothesis is rejected after Holm correction. Three calls reduce paired success by 4.17 points under neutral labels and 2.13 points under role labels, while raising API-equivalent valuation by factors of 2.67 and 2.78. An earlier 1,000-assignment GPT-5.4-mini study on the same 200 task IDs produces effects in the same direction for all four factorial contrasts, although none of the four tests rejects after correction. The negative call-structure result persists under a prespecified source-dependence audit and in a separate analysis of 800 newly sampled task IDs. Exploratory direct-solver differences from the one-call workflows are small, with intervals crossing zero; the direct solver has higher success on observed task pairs than both three-call workflows. In a separate 2,000-block comparison, the adapted Self-Collaboration controller has lower success on observed task pairs and approximately three times the valuation, but infrastructure-related missingness prevents estimation of its quality difference for the full assigned population. For the tested models and full-context function-generation tasks, role labels showed no reliable benefit; on complete eligible task pairs, three-call workflows cost more and had lower executable success than matched one-call workflows.
\end{abstract}
