\title[Role Labels and Call Topology]{Role Labels or Extra Calls? A Controlled Study of Quality and Cost in LLM Code Generation}

\begin{abstract}
LLM code-generation workflows often combine named roles with multiple model calls, making it unclear which design choice changes quality and resource use. We disentangle these choices in a crossed experiment: neutral versus planner--implementer--reviewer labels, and one versus three calls, with identical operations and full task context; a direct solver is a fifth condition. GPT-5.6 Luna receives 15,000 assignments on 1,000 BigCodeBench tasks, with three fresh repeats per condition. Of 14,961 completed candidates, 985 tasks pass reference controls. Role labels change success by $-0.75$ percentage points in one call and $+1.35$ points in three calls; neither comparison rejects after Holm correction. Three calls reduce paired success by 4.17 points under neutral labels and 2.13 points under role labels, while raising API-equivalent valuation by factors of 2.67 and 2.78. An earlier 1,000-assignment GPT-5.4-mini study on the reused 200 task IDs has the same sign for all four factorial contrasts, although none rejects after correction. A prespecified source-dependence audit and a separate analysis of 800 newly sampled task IDs preserve the negative topology result. Exploratory direct-solver differences from the one-call workflows are small with intervals crossing zero; its observed paired success is higher than both three-call workflows. A separate 2,000-block comparison with an adapted Self-Collaboration controller gives lower observed-pair success and approximately three times the valuation, but infrastructure missingness leaves its full-population quality difference unidentified. These results provide evidence for a boundary case: for the tested models and full-context function-generation workflow, role labels alone show no robust benefit, while extra call boundaries increase cost and reduce executable success.
\end{abstract}
