\section{Repeated factorial study on 1,000 tasks}
\label{sec:scale-results}
\subsection{Design and completed evaluation}
The larger study retains the five conditions D, SN, SR, MN and MR and the full supplied task context. It uses GPT-5.6 Luna with medium reasoning through Codex CLI 0.153.4. Each condition receives three fresh generations per task, giving 15,000 assignments across 1,000 task clusters and 27,000 planned calls. The repeat labels identify fresh requests; they do not set a provider sampling seed. Model-call deadlines are 600 seconds. Neither context compression nor repair based on benchmark test results is allowed. The task clusters are the task-level units; 264 quality endpoints remain unknown, and the four contrasts use complete-pair task estimators with 982, 964, 968 and 971 task pairs, respectively.

The allocation contains the 200 tasks used in the initial factorial study and 800 additional tasks from the original randomized order, excluding development tasks and the separate layout study. All programs are generated afresh; earlier answers are not pooled with them. Thus this is a larger experiment with a different requested model, not 1,000 newly held-out tasks or a replication with immutable model weights. Reference and incorrect-program controls, completed before generation, make 985 tasks eligible for quality measurement. Task similarity and cross-study overlap are described separately below.

Generation produced 14,961 complete candidates; 39 submitted assignments remained incomplete. Every complete candidate underwent the native BigCodeBench evaluation. The analysis retains all 15,000 assigned rows, including 264 unknown quality endpoints arising from generation or control availability. Unknown endpoints remain distinct from test failures. Table~\ref{tab:scale-quality} separates generation completion from quality observability; three attempts at one task are not treated as three independent task units.
\input{sections/scale1000_quality_en}

For each of four prespecified contrasts, both conditions must have all three quality endpoints available. The difference between their three-repeat means is calculated within each task, and tasks receive equal weight. The four contrasts therefore use their respective complete-pair task counts. The primary two-sided $t$-tests form one four-test Holm family at $\alpha=0.05$. The 95\% task-bootstrap intervals are descriptive, with 10,000 resamples and seed 20260909; they do not replace the multiplicity correction. The experiment estimates per-generation test success, not success after choosing the best of three candidates. Appendix~\ref{app:expanded-studies} gives the calculations and missingness rules.

\subsection{Roles, call structure and quality}
\input{sections/scale1000_contrasts_en}
Within one call, the role-minus-neutral difference is $-0.75$ percentage points, with a 95\% interval of $[-1.97,0.44]$. Within three calls it is $+1.35$ points, with interval $[0.17,2.56]$. The latter interval excludes zero, but its Holm-adjusted $p=0.0599$ does not meet the specified familywise threshold. Neither role-label comparison therefore supports a rejection in the primary family.

Splitting the stages across three calls reduces observed test success relative to keeping them within one response: by 4.17 points for neutral instructions and 2.13 points for role-labelled instructions. Both comparisons reject after Holm adjustment. Their full-assignment identification bounds also remain below zero (Table~\ref{tab:scale-contrasts}); this particular direction does not depend on assigning favourable values to missing binary outcomes. These bounds address missingness, not sampling uncertainty or model drift.

The descriptive role-by-call-count interaction is $+2.08$ points, with interval $[0.42,3.74]$, on the common 963-task subset. This describes how the two role contrasts differ on the same tasks; it is outside the four-test family. None of the four primary task-difference vectors is degenerate. The design includes no justified non-inferiority margin or joint quality--cost decision, so the intervals do not establish quality preservation.

\subsection{Resource use and robustness}
\input{sections/scale1000_resources_en}
Single-call roles have a valuation ratio of 0.939 relative to neutral stages, or 6.08\% lower API-equivalent valuation on 997 complete three-repeat task pairs. The token ratio is 0.986. Within three calls, the corresponding valuation ratio is 0.978 and the token ratio 0.990 on 979 task pairs. These resource differences are descriptive; their magnitude is smaller than the initial mini study's single-call difference.

The resource change associated with call structure is much larger. Three-call neutral instructions require 3.08 times the tokens and 2.67 times the valuation of single-call neutral instructions. With roles, the ratios are 3.09 and 2.78. Removing the cache discount leaves valuation ratios of 2.78 and 2.87, respectively. These values concern the implemented full-context workflows and published API-equivalent rates, not subscription charges or total research costs. Token counts include repeated inputs and exposed intermediate answers; provider reasoning is not added twice.

The supplementary source-group bootstrap preserves the task-weighted point estimates. Under the combined instruction/code similarity rule, the single-call role interval is $[-1.99,0.47]$ points and the three-call role interval $[0.14,2.60]$. The topology intervals remain negative: $[-5.69,-2.72]$ for neutral instructions and $[-3.44,-0.79]$ with roles. These intervals use the predefined groups and are sensitivity results, not another set of significance tests. Similarity screening cannot establish semantic independence or exclude training contamination.

The larger experiment supports a protocol-specific architectural conclusion: for these tasks and settings, transmitting the stages across additional model calls costs more and lowers test success. It does not establish a general claim about multi-agent systems. It also does not isolate a universal benefit of role names: the label effects remain small, sensitive to the surrounding instructions and unconfirmed in the corrected quality tests.
