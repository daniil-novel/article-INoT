\title[Hybrid-INoT: internal roles and external verification]{Hybrid-INoT: internal role segregation and external verification for code generation}
\author*[1]{\fnm{Daniil} \sur{Privezentsev}}\email{daprivezentsev@edu.hse.ru}
\affil*[1]{\orgname{National Research University Higher School of Economics}, \orgaddress{\city{Moscow}, \country{Russia}}}

\abstract{Hybrid-INoT combines planning, implementation and critique within one model response with external executable verification. We formalize its single-pass core and test role-labelled versus neutral instructions crossed with one versus three model calls, holding the operations and full task context fixed. A direct solver is the fifth condition. In the largest completed study, GPT-5.6 Luna with medium reasoning receives 15,000 assignments on 1,000 BigCodeBench tasks with three repeats per condition. All 14,961 complete candidates undergo native evaluation; 985 tasks pass reference controls. Observed success ranges from 50.07\% to 54.59\%. Role-minus-neutral quality differences are $-0.75$ percentage points within one call and $+1.35$ within three calls; neither rejects after the four-test Holm correction. Three-call execution lowers quality by 4.17 points for neutral instructions and 2.13 points for role-labelled instructions, while requiring 2.67 and 2.78 times their single-call API-equivalent valuations. Whole-task and source-group intervals, repeated-outcome tables and explicit missingness support these comparisons. The earlier 200-task factorial and 80-task label-by-layout studies retain their separate model and sampling scopes. Together, the findings identify a cost of the tested call structure without establishing a general quality benefit from role names, quality non-inferiority, or independent internal agents.}

\keywords{Hybrid-INoT, internal role segregation, external verification, token efficiency, factorial design, code generation}

\section{Initial factorial study with GPT-5.4 mini}
\label{sec:primary-results}
\subsection{Allocation, observed outcomes and missingness}
All 1,000 assignments and 1,800 planned turns were submitted. The original attempt completed 457 of 460 submitted cells; the separately frozen continuation completed 539 of 540 untouched cells. Four cells remain incomplete: D on /1028 and SN on /388 after transport errors, SN on /249 after a deadline in the original attempt, and SN on /1028 after the continuation deadline. The final interrupted stream includes a completed response and usage, but its terminal status violates the 180-second acceptance criterion. It is therefore not used as an observed program. Event arrival times are not independently timestamped, and no timing sequence beyond the recorded terminal result is inferred.

The native evaluator checked all 996 observed programs. After the common reference gate, 963 observations remain evaluable: 453 passes, 507 failures and three native timeouts. The other 33 observed programs belong to reference-ineligible tasks. Two final-format extraction failures, D /494 and MR /100, remain empty observed programs with their original responses retained. Generation failures, native timeouts, format errors and unavailable controls are distinct in the published records and in the complete task appendix.

Table~\ref{tab:heldout-quality} reports observed rates and extreme all-assignment ranges. Across the five primary conditions, observed test-success rates range from 44.56\% to 48.70\%, giving substantial room for both improvement and deterioration. The lower SN denominator reflects two missing generations on otherwise eligible tasks; both /1028 missing generations coincide with an unavailable reference control. The INoT* row belongs to a separate exploratory batch, discussed below.

\subsection{Four predeclared quality contrasts}
Table~\ref{tab:heldout-tests} and Fig.~\ref{fig:quality-effects} show paired differences. Single-call roles produce six wins and eight losses against neutral stages on 191 eligible pairs. Three-call roles produce ten wins and eight losses on 193 pairs. Neither comparison provides evidence of improved quality after correction. The two topology contrasts also fail to reject: the smallest adjusted $p$ is 0.3134 for MN versus SN. The descriptive difference between the three-call and one-call role contrasts is +2.09 percentage points, with interval [-4.19,+8.38], on the common 191-task subset.

These findings do not establish equivalent quality. In particular, the interval for SR minus SN still includes a loss of 4.71 points. The resource reduction therefore does not establish savings with preserved quality. Failure to reject the other contrasts similarly leaves improvements and losses compatible with the observed uncertainty.

\subsection{Measured resources and price sensitivity}
Completed candidates use 8,569,456 tokens and have total valuation USD 14.1467676. The submitted-turn ledger additionally retains 15,729 known tokens from the interrupted continuation call. Across all submitted calls, known usage is therefore 8,585,185 tokens: 5,776,714 input, including 4,097,536 cached, and 2,808,471 output, including 2,076,742 reasoning. The 1,797 calls with counters are valued at USD 14.2048182, or USD 16.970655 without cache discounts. Three interrupted calls have unknown usage; the known total is not a complete account of consumption. None of these valuations is a subscription charge.

Table~\ref{tab:heldout-resources} reports per-condition means over completed candidates. The paired comparisons in Table~\ref{tab:heldout-resource-contrasts} use a common subset for each resource contrast. SR versus SN uses 197 pairs: 8.76\% fewer tokens and 18.45\% lower valuation, with a mean USD difference of -0.0019761 [-0.0027617,-0.0012296]. The no-cache valuation remains 16.21\% lower. Thus the observed label-associated resource difference does not disappear when the cache discount is removed, although its magnitude changes. These resource intervals are descriptive and outside the four quality tests.

Within three calls, the MR/MN valuation ratio is 0.985 and its mean-difference interval crosses zero. The single-call resource finding therefore does not generalize clearly across topologies. Conversely, MN/SN and MR/SR use 2.84 and 3.06 times as many tokens and cost 2.09 and 2.50 times as much; no-cache valuation ratios are 2.19 and 2.56. Full repeated inputs and intermediate responses form part of this measured expense. \section{Exploratory comparators and development replication}
Direct solving has the lowest observed token and valuation means among the five main conditions. SN minus D has a paired quality difference of zero [-4.19,+4.19] on 191 tasks, but a valuation ratio of 1.632 on 197 resource pairs. MR minus D has a quality difference of -3.11 [-7.77,+1.04] and costs 3.359 times as much on 199 resource pairs. These descriptive comparisons support keeping a direct baseline; they do not establish statistical dominance.

The separately dispatched INoT* adaptation yields 199 complete programs from 200 assignments. One assignment reaches the original deadline; none fails final-format extraction. On 192 eligible outcomes, 96 pass (50\%); all-assignment bounds are 48--52\%. Known usage is 767,679 tokens and USD 1.05886035 valuation (USD 1.33326675 without cache discounts), with one call lacking usage. Relative to D, its paired quality difference is +1.04 [-3.65,+5.73] and its valuation ratio is 0.805; relative to SR the corresponding values are +2.08 [-2.08,+6.25] and 0.600. Table~\ref{tab:heldout-exploratory} retains different quality/resource pair counts. Because this algorithm adaptation ran in a separate batch, timing, cache and provider changes remain potential confounders. It is not an author-code replication or part of the four-test family. \section{Discussion}
The larger factorial study separates two findings. Call structure has a substantial resource effect and, in these settings, a quality cost: the three-call variants consume more and pass fewer benchmark tests than the corresponding single-call variants. The smaller role-label differences do not support corrected quality rejections. A shared response can therefore be a useful implementation choice without its named roles providing an independently demonstrated quality benefit.

The initial mini study and the separate label-by-layout experiment retain their own samples, execution settings and statistical families. The initial single-call role-associated valuation reduction is larger than the Luna result; the minimal label-by-layout study does not convincingly reproduce that reduction. These comparisons limit claims about robustness. They are not a matched experiment on model identity or a formal test that an effect changed across studies. Repeated calls within a task improve its success-rate estimate but do not add new task units or resolve public-benchmark training overlap.

The theoretical resource balance remains conditional. Repeated context transmission is one measurable expense, while output length, provider reasoning and caching also respond to the instructions. The experiment therefore measures the implemented configurations rather than an optimal allocation under a common hard inference budget. It does not show that multiple calls cannot help in an interactive repository setting.

An algorithmic instruction such as INoT* and a minimal role-label substitution are different interventions. The former changes the reasoning procedure; SCC additionally changes interaction and generated-test feedback. Their comparisons must retain those scopes. Likewise, benchmark test success measures the specified executable contract. The development cases show why prompt--test agreement deserves separate scrutiny, and their outcomes are retained rather than excluded after inspection.

\section{Conclusion}
Hybrid-INoT organizes planning, implementation and critique within a shared model response and keeps executable verification external. Its conditional token balance motivates reducing repeated context transmission, while the component experiments test the resulting implementation without compression or benchmark-driven reruns. In the 1,000-task, three-repeat Luna study, all 14,961 complete programs are natively evaluated. Splitting the stages across three calls lowers test success by 2.13--4.17 percentage points and increases API-equivalent valuation by factors of 2.67--2.78. Role-label quality effects do not pass the four-test Holm correction. The earlier factorial and isolated label-by-layout studies narrow the robustness claim further. The supported contribution is an explicit architecture and a large component evaluation that identifies where its resource rationale holds and where its proposed role benefits remain unconfirmed. Adaptive routing, selective reruns and multi-repository repair require separate evaluation.

\section*{Research materials}
The study uses the public BigCodeBench and SWE-bench benchmarks cited above. The appendices contain the experimental instructions, the outcome of every assigned task in the completed function-generation studies, and additional procedural details.
